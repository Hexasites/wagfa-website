Every tagged fish adds to what scientists know about game fish. Here's how WA anglers take part.

The Gamefish Tagging Program

The largest program of its kind in the world

The Gamefish Tagging Program has run since 1973. It is the largest saltwater tagging program of its kind in the world.

Game fishers collect information on the biology of billfish, tunas, sharks and sport fish: their distribution, movement, growth and exploitation. In doing so they take part in managing the fishery.

Fish tags are supplied by NSW fisheries authorities to members of registered fishing clubs affiliated with the Game Fishing Association of Australia. WAGFA member clubs are affiliated with GFAA through WAGFA.

How a tag works

  • Every tag carries a number and a return address on its streamer.
  • Each tag comes attached to a numbered, prepaid card showing where to place it in the fish.
  • Tags usually go in the shoulder of a fish brought alongside the boat.
  • The card records the date and location, the boat, angler and captain, and the fish's estimated size and condition.

Get involved

How to take part

  1. Join a club

    Tags go to members of GFAA-affiliated clubs, so start with a WAGFA member club.

  2. Get your tags

    Ask your club how it distributes tags and cards to members.

  3. Tag by the rules

    GFAA Angling Rules apply until the fish is tagged. Tag poles must not exceed 4.57 m (15 ft), and the pole must be in hand when the fish is tagged.

  4. Send in the card

    Fill in the prepaid card and post it promptly so the release is recorded.

Read the full GFAA Release Fishing rules before you tag.

Measure & release

Records you don’t have to keep

GFAA recognises length records for all tackle, so a fish can make the record book and swim away.

  • Measure-and-release catches must be logged on an approved GFAA card.
  • A fish may only be released after measuring if it is healthy and vigorous.
  • Juniors can score release points in the GFAA National Junior Tournament, which counts tagged and measured fish.

WA initiatives

Science close to home

Tag & Release Shark Initiative

Perth Game Fishing Club's tagging initiative aims to understand the movement patterns, behaviour and biology of shark species using tagging technology.

More from PGFC

Metropolitan FAD program

Since 1997 PGFC has run Perth's metropolitan fish aggregating devices, which attract tuna, yellowtail kingfish, mahi mahi, wahoo and marlin offshore.
